📌 Project Conclusion
PowerShock suggests that renewable energy feed-in data can provide useful early-warning signals for selected energy-sensitive DAX stocks. Granger Causality analysis confirms that past feed-in levels statistically predict volatility for Siemens (1–2 days ahead) and E.ON (2 days ahead). However, the signal is not strong enough to be used alone for trading decisions — low R² values confirm that macro conditions, earnings events, and broader market dynamics also drive significant volatility.

This dashboard is better suited as a market-monitoring and risk-intelligence tool — one that adds a novel energy-data dimension to equity risk analysis, particularly for utilities and industrial companies with high electricity exposure.

Granger Causality
Can renewable energy shocks predict future stock volatility?iGranger Causality tests whether past values of one variable (renewable feed-in) help predict future values of another (stock volatility), beyond what past volatility alone predicts. It is predictive, not structural causation.
What is Granger Causality?
If past values of renewable feed-in X help predict current stock volatility Y — beyond what past volatility alone predicts — then X is said to Granger-cause Y.
10:00 AM · Low wind output detected in SMARD data
11:00 AM · Wholesale electricity price rises
Next Day · Siemens/E.ON volatility increases
Important: Granger causality is predictive, not structural. It does not prove that feed-in mechanistically drives volatility through a specific causal pathway — only that past feed-in data improves the forecast.

How feed-in shocks may reach stock markets through wholesale electricity pricing
⏳ EPEX data not yet connected
Why EPEX Spot Prices Are the Missing Link
The European Power Exchange (EPEX) sets hourly wholesale electricity prices in Germany. When renewable generation falls short, gas and coal plants — which operate at significantly higher marginal cost — fill the gap. This may push spot prices upward via the Merit-Order Effect: when cheaper plants are exhausted, progressively more expensive sources set the price.
⚡ Energy Consumers (BMW, MBG, VOW3, SIE)
Higher spot prices may increase production input costs, compress operating margins, and create earnings uncertainty — this may appear as elevated stock volatility.
🔋 Energy Generators (RWE, EOAN)
Price spikes create revenue uncertainty and may attract speculative trading — raising stock volatility even for companies that may benefit from higher selling prices.
📋 Methodology — Once EPEX Data Is Connected

5. Compare whether feed-in + price model outperforms feed-in-only model
